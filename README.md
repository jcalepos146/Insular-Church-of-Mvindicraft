## Ecclesial Identity

The Insular Church is presented as a distinct **sui iuris Western Church** in full ecclesial communion with the restored Papacy. It is neither an independent denomination nor simply a Latin diocese. It preserves its own received Insular liturgy, calendar, canon law, synodal institutions, hierarchy, and regional customs while recognizing the universal Petrine primacy of the Bishop of Rome.

Ordinary government within the Isles is exercised through the **Regional Synod** and the **College of Five Primates**, according to the Church's proper law and always within communion with Rome.

### The Five Primatial Sees

- **Armagh** — Primate of Ireland; senior Insular see and customary first among equals
- **St Davids** — Primate of Wales
- **Canterbury** — Primate of Southern England
- **York** — Primate of Northern England
- **St Andrews** — Primate of Scotland

Armagh normally convokes the common Regional Synod, while the individual primacies retain their historic ceremonial and regional precedence.

## Creeds and Doctrinal Symbols Received

The Insular Church professes the historic creeds and doctrinal formularies of the ancient and medieval Western Church as authoritative expressions of the Catholic faith. In particular, it receives:

- **The Apostles' Creed** — the ancient baptismal symbol of the Western Church
- **The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed** — the common confession of faith received from Nicaea I and Constantinople I
- **The Athanasian Creed (*Quicumque Vult*)** — received as a venerable Western exposition of Trinitarian and Christological orthodoxy
- **The Definition of Chalcedon** — received as the authoritative Christological definition of the Council of Chalcedon
- **The Tome of St Leo** — received in accordance with its approbation at Chalcedon and as a principal Western witness to orthodox Christology

These symbols are interpreted in continuity with Sacred Scripture, the Fathers, the received councils of the Church, and the living communion of the Insular Church with the Roman See.

### Principal Particular Synods Received

The Insular Church also receives a number of historically important **local and regional synods** whose teaching, discipline, or ecclesiastical settlement entered into the received patrimony of the Western Church. These are honored according to their proper rank and are not placed on the same level as ecumenical councils.

1. **Carthage** — 418 — received especially for its anti-Pelagian teaching on original sin, grace, and infant baptism
2. **Orange II** — 529 — received for its teaching on the priority and necessity of divine grace against Semi-Pelagianism
3. **Toledo III** — 589 — received as a major Western settlement of Nicene orthodoxy and the conversion of the Visigothic kingdom
4. **Whitby** — 664 — received as an important settlement in the ordering of the Insular churches toward common Roman observance
5. **Frankfurt** — 794 — received for its importance in Carolingian ecclesiastical reform, with its acts interpreted in harmony with Nicaea II
6. **Quierzy** — 853 — received as an important Western witness in the ninth-century disputes concerning grace and predestination
7. **Valence** — 855 — received for its Augustinian emphasis on grace, divine foreknowledge, and predestination
8. **Clermont** — 1095 — received as a major reforming council of the medieval Western Church and an important expression of papal leadership

Their authority is understood as **particular and received**, rather than intrinsically ecumenical: they are accepted insofar as their orthodox doctrine and legitimate discipline have been confirmed, incorporated, or consistently received within the wider Catholic tradition.

### The Council of Constance — 1414–1418

The Insular Church receives Constance as the great council by which the Western Schism was healed and ecclesial order restored. It also understands the council's extraordinary emergency legislation as evidence of a **providential state of exception** within the life of the Church.

In a grave and manifest crisis in which the ordinary exercise of papal government becomes impossible, doubtful, or incapable of preserving visible ecclesial unity, the Church may act through extraordinary conciliar means to preserve the faith, maintain apostolic succession, and restore normal ecclesial order. Such authority is **remedial and exceptional rather than ordinary**: it exists from necessity, not as a rival principle to Roman primacy, and ceases once a lawful and recognizable exercise of Petrine government has been restored.

Thus, in the Insular interpretation, Constance does not abolish or subordinate the papal office as the normal constitution of the Western Church. Rather, it demonstrates that Divine Providence furnished the Church with an emergency means of survival for her darkest hour.

## Historical Setting

The website reflects a late-medieval Mvndicraft setting in which an earlier breakdown of Roman ecclesial order produced an extraordinary vacancy-era settlement in the British Isles and Ireland. That emergency period has now ended with the restoration of a lawful Bishop of Rome and the reconciliation of the Insular Church with the Roman See.

Older documents preserved in the `docs/` directory may therefore contain **vacancy-era or pre-restoration language**. These texts are retained as historical and canonical archives. Where their former emergency provisions conflict with the present settlement, they are to be read in light of the Church's restored communion with Rome and its current sui iuris constitution.
