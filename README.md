## Ecclesial Identity

The Insular Church is presented as a distinct **sui iuris Western Church** in full ecclesial communion with the restored Papacy. It is neither an independent denomination nor simply a Latin diocese. It preserves its own received Insular liturgy, calendar, canon law, synodal institutions, hierarchy, and regional customs while recognizing the universal Petrine primacy of the Bishop of Rome.

Ordinary government within the Isles is exercised through the **Regional Synod** and the **College of Five Primates**, according to the Church's proper law and always within communion with Rome.

### The Five Primatial Sees

- **Armagh** — Primate of Ireland; senior Insular see and customary first among equals
- **St Davids** — Primate of Wales
- **Canterbury** — Primate of Southern England
- **York** — Primate of Northern England
- **St Andrews** — Primate of Scotland

Armagh normally convokes the common Regional Synod, while the individual primacies retain their historic ceremonial and regional precedence.

## Councils Received

The Insular Church receives the doctrinal inheritance of the ancient undivided Church together with the principal general councils of the medieval Western Church that form its theological and constitutional patrimony.

### The Council of Constance — 1414–1418

The Insular Church receives Constance as the great council by which the Western Schism was healed and ecclesial order restored. It also understands the council's extraordinary emergency legislation as evidence of a **providential state of exception** within the life of the Church.

In a grave and manifest crisis in which the ordinary exercise of papal government becomes impossible, doubtful, or incapable of preserving visible ecclesial unity, the Church may act through extraordinary conciliar means to preserve the faith, maintain apostolic succession, and restore normal ecclesial order. Such authority is **remedial and exceptional rather than ordinary**: it exists from necessity, not as a rival principle to Roman primacy, and ceases once a lawful and recognizable exercise of Petrine government has been restored.

Thus, in the Insular interpretation, Constance does not abolish or subordinate the papal office as the normal constitution of the Western Church. Rather, it demonstrates that Divine Providence furnished the Church with an emergency means of survival for her darkest hour.

## Historical Setting

The website reflects a late-medieval Mvindicraft setting in which an earlier breakdown of Roman ecclesial order produced an extraordinary vacancy-era settlement in the British Isles and Ireland. That emergency period has now ended with the restoration of a lawful Bishop of Rome and the reconciliation of the Insular Church with the Roman See.

Older documents preserved in the `docs/` directory may therefore contain **vacancy-era or pre-restoration language**. These texts are retained as historical and canonical archives. Where their former emergency provisions conflict with the present settlement, they are to be read in light of the Church's restored communion with Rome and its current sui iuris constitution.
