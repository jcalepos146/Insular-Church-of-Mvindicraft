Current Ecclesial Status

The Insular Church of Mvndicraft is a sui iuris Western Church in full communion with the restored Roman See. It professes the Catholic faith, recognises the Bishop of Rome as Roman Pontiff and universal Petrine primate, and preserves as its own patrimony the ancient Christian inheritance of Ireland, Scotland, Wales, and England.

Within that communion, the Church possesses its own Insular Rite, liturgical calendar, canon law, clerical discipline, Regional Synod, and College of Four Primates. Ordinary internal government belongs to the bishops and synodal institutions of the Insular Church according to its proper law, while communion in faith and the universal ministry of unity belong to the restored Papacy.

The Church is presently ordered through four national primatial sees: Ireland — Armagh, Scotland — St Andrews, Wales / the Romano-British Kingdom — St Davids, and the Holy Britannian Empire of the Saxon Nation — Canterbury. Armagh is first among equals and serves as the senior Insular see. The present constitutional settlement is therefore neither sedevacantist nor separatist: the emergency ecclesiology of the former Western crisis has ceased, and the Church now understands its autonomy as a lawful expression of Catholic communion rather than a substitute for Rome.

The Church receives the first seven Ecumenical Councils, the four Lateran Councils, and the Council of Constance as part of its conciliar inheritance. Constance is additionally received as evidence of a providential state of exception: in the Church's darkest hour, when ordinary papal government is gravely obstructed or doubtful, extraordinary conciliar action may preserve faith, succession, and visible unity until normal Petrine government can be restored. Such authority is exceptional, remedial, and terminates with restoration.

History of the Western Crisis

The modern Insular Church took its present institutional form during a crisis of legitimacy in the Western Church. Across the first four versions of the Mvndicraft server, a valid line of Apostolic Succession was maintained from Pope Geerus IX through to Pope Anastasius III. The Petrine office was held by men who had been duly ordained, and the sacraments flowed through an unbroken chain of consecration stretching back to the founding of the server in the Year of Our Lord 2020.

When International Organisations were established in a later version of Mvndicraft, the political landscape shifted. The Lombard King of Italy — a temporal ruler whose kingdom is now defunct and powerless — took it upon himself to appoint a new pope: Pope John I. John I was neither an ordained bishop nor even a priest. His elevation was a purely political act, proclaimed by temporal authority rather than conferred through the sacrament of Holy Orders.

The Western Crisis and the Restoration of Rome

Because John I lacked valid ordination, appointments descending from his line were regarded by the Insular bishops as gravely defective, producing a prolonged crisis of legitimacy throughout Western Christendom. Rival claimants gathered political support, while the churches of the Isles preserved their own episcopal succession, liturgical life, and synodal government until a lawful restoration of the Roman See could be achieved.

That extraordinary period has now ended. With the restoration of a validly ordained Bishop of Rome and the reconciliation of the surviving Western churches, the Insular Church has entered full ecclesial communion with the restored Papacy. The earlier emergency claims of vacancy belonged to a particular historical crisis and no longer describe the Church's present position.

The Founding of the Insular Church

In the face of this collapse, the faithful of Britain and Ireland gathered to preserve the ancient tradition. Drawing upon the heritage of the pre-Tridentine Insular Rite — the traditions of Saints Patrick, Columba, Aidan, Cuthbert, Hilda, and Bede — the Insular Church was established under the governance of a College of Four Primates, headed by the See of Armagh as first among equals. The First Insular Synod was convened in the City of Armagh, where the Canons that now govern the Church were promulgated and signed by every Archbishop, Bishop, and Abbot present.

The Insular Church holds to the doctrine of Apostolic Succession and recognises the Petrine ministry entrusted to the Bishop of Rome. Following the restoration of the Papacy, the Church was received into full communion as a sui iuris Western Church: self-governing in its proper liturgical, disciplinary, and regional affairs, while remaining united to the Roman Pontiff in faith and ecclesial communion. Its historic synodal institutions were therefore preserved rather than abolished.

A Sui Iuris Church of the Western Communion

The restored settlement recognises the Insular Church as a distinct sui iuris Western Church possessing its own received rite, calendar, canon law, synodal institutions, and hierarchy. The Roman Pontiff exercises the universal Petrine primacy of the reunited Western Church, while ordinary government within the Isles is exercised according to Insular law by the bishops and the Regional Synod. The Church therefore combines communion with Rome with a deliberately strong tradition of local synodality and liturgical patrimony.

Its proper territory is ordered through four constituent nations and their primatial sees: Ireland (Armagh), Scotland (St Andrews), Wales / the Romano-British Kingdom (St Davids), and the Holy Britannian Empire of the Saxon Nation (Canterbury). Armagh is first among equals and customary convener of the common Synod. The Church's vocation is not to replace the wider Western Church, but to preserve and develop the ancient Christian inheritance of the Isles within the communion of the restored Roman See.

Beliefs & Doctrine

The Insular Church holds to the undivided faith of the early centuries, guided by the Revised Confession of Faith contained within the Bishops' Book as written by Archbishop Corpo MacAirthir. The Church recognises only those saints canonised before A.D. 1550 and celebrates the liturgy according to the Pre-Tridentine Insular Rite.

  • Apostolic Succession — The Church holds that valid Holy Orders require genuine episcopal succession from the Apostles and preserves the succession maintained through the Western crisis.
  • Communion with the Restored Papacy — The Insular Church recognises the validly restored Bishop of Rome as Roman Pontiff and visible principle of communion for the Western Church, while retaining its own lawful sui iuris institutions.
  • The Authority of Scripture — The Douai-Rheims-Challoner Bible serves as the authoritative vernacular translation; the Latin Vulgate of St. Jerome as the official Latin text (Canon IX of the Synod of Armagh).
  • Regional Synodal Governance — The Regional Synod governs common Insular affairs according to the Church's own canon law, subject to the bonds of faith and communion owed to the Roman Pontiff. Decisions ordinarily require a two-thirds majority; matters touching the Church's received doctrine or constitution require the consent of all four Primates and communion with Rome.
  • The College of Primates — The Church presently has four national Primatial Sees: Armagh (Ireland), St. Andrews (Scotland), St. Davids (Wales / the Romano-British Kingdom), and Canterbury (the Holy Britannian Empire of the Saxon Nation). Armagh is first among equals.
  • Sui Iuris Western Identity — The Church is neither a separate denomination nor a Latin diocese. It is a self-governing Western Church in communion with Rome, preserving its own Insular liturgy, calendar, discipline, and synodal institutions.
  • Valid Ordination — For an ordination to be valid, it must have proper matter, proper formula, be performed by an ordained bishop of good standing, and carry proper intent of both parties (Canon XII).
  • The Mvndi-Seasons Calendar — The Church orders its liturgical year according to a 28-day calendar of four seasons of seven days each, developed by Robert MacInerney and adopted by synodal decree.

The Council of Constance

  1. Constance A.D. 1414–1418

Constance and the state of exception. The Insular Church receives the Council of Constance as the great council by which the Western Schism was healed and ecclesial order restored. In particular, it understands the council’s emergency legislation as a providential safeguard placed within the constitution of the Church: when a grave and manifest crisis renders the ordinary exercise of papal government impossible, doubtful, or incapable of preserving visible ecclesial unity, the Church may act in an extraordinary conciliar state of exception for the preservation of the faith, the succession of lawful pastors, and the restoration of normal order. This authority is remedial rather than ordinary, exists for necessity rather than rivalry with the Roman See, and ceases once a lawful and universally recognizable exercise of Petrine primacy has been restored. Thus Constance is received not as a denial of papal primacy, but as evidence that Divine Providence has furnished the Church with an emergency means of survival in her darkest hour.

Canons of the Synod of Armagh

Promulgated by the First Insular Synod in the City of Armagh

Canon I

On the Convening of Synods

The right of convening a Synod rests solely with the Primates. Each Archbishop, Bishop, and Abbot has one vote. Temporal princes may attend and counsel but may not vote. Decisions require two-thirds majority; dogma and doctrine require unanimity of the Primates.

Canon II

On the Authority of Synods

The Regional Synod is the highest ordinary authority for matters common to the Insular Church, exercising that authority within the Church's sui iuris competence and in communion with the Roman Pontiff. Diocesan matters belong to the local bishop; provincial matters ordinarily belong to the bishops and Primate of the province.

Canon III

On the Hierarchy of Primacies

The Church is headed by a college of four national Primates: Armagh (Ireland, first among equals), St. Andrews (Scotland), St. Davids (Wales / the Romano-British Kingdom), and Canterbury (the Holy Britannian Empire of the Saxon Nation). Armagh holds seniority of honor among the four sees.

Canon IV

On the Bishops and their Dioceses

No town shall have more than one bishop. Bishops alone ordain priests within their diocese and are responsible for the conduct, behaviour, and doctrinal soundness of all clergy in their jurisdiction.

Canon V

On the Role of Temporal Princes in Ecclesiastical Affairs

Kings and princes may present candidates for vacant Sees within their jurisdiction and raise objections on specific grounds (disobedience, heresy, banishment, treason, or schism), but final determination rests with the provincial Primate.

Canon VI

On Relations with other Communions

The Church is in full communion with the restored Roman See and with those churches sharing that communion. Historic relations with Constantinople and other apostolic churches are to be conducted with honour and charity according to the discipline of the wider communion. Valid baptisms are recognised according to the received sacramental doctrine of the Church.

Canon VII

On Relations with Heathens, Hebrews, and Mohammedans

Relations with heathens are left to local authorities. Hebrews may traverse and trade in peace. Mohammedans may trade, travel, and engage in diplomacy; relations are determined principally by their treatment of Christians in their own lands.

Canon VIII

On the Establishment of a Missionary Order

The establishment and confirmation of religious orders proper to the Insular Church belongs to the Regional Synod and the four Primates according to Insular canon law, while preserving any rights of confirmation or recognition reserved to the Roman Pontiff by the terms of communion.

Canon IX

On the matter of Holy Scripture

The Douai-Rheims-Challoner version serves as the authoritative vernacular Bible. The Vulgate of St. Jerome serves as the official Latin Bible of the Church.

Canon X

On the Adoption of the Mvndi-Seasons Calendar

The Church adopts the 28-day calendar based on Mvndicraft's seven-day seasons. All liturgical observances are ordered accordingly. Local saints' feasts are at the discretion of bishops.

Canon XI

On the Adoption of the Bishop's Book

The Revised Confession of Faith, written by Corpo MacAirthir and contained within the Bishops' Book, stands as the foundational teaching on all matters of doctrine and dogma.

Canon XII

On the Ordination of Clergy from other Communions

Candidates received from churches outside full communion must profess the Catholic faith as received by the Insular Church. The Regional Synod regulates the reception or ordination of clergy from other communions in accordance with sacramental validity, pastoral prudence, and the norms of communion with Rome. A valid ordination requires proper matter, proper form, a validly consecrated bishop, and the requisite sacramental intent.

Current Ecclesial Order

Post-Restoration Settlement

One communion with Rome; one Insular patrimony; four national primatial sees.

The restoration of the Roman See has ended the former emergency order. The following principles describe the ordinary constitutional life of the Insular Church today.

Communion with the Roman Pontiff

The Bishop of Rome is recognised as Roman Pontiff, successor of St Peter, and universal visible principle of communion. The Insular Church does not claim an independent universal jurisdiction and no longer treats the Roman See as vacant.

Sui Iuris Government

The Insular Church governs its proper liturgical, disciplinary, administrative, and regional affairs through its own bishops, canon law, College of Primates, and Regional Synod, in accordance with the terms of communion with Rome.

The Four National Primatial Sees

Armagh serves Ireland and is first among equals. St Andrews serves Scotland. St Davids serves Wales and the Romano-British Kingdom. Canterbury serves the Holy Britannian Empire of the Saxon Nation. Together these four sees constitute the present primatial structure of the Insular Church.

The Insular Rite

The Church preserves and develops its pre-Tridentine Western liturgical patrimony, including its own calendar, lectionary, ordinal, ceremonial customs, and local saints, as the proper rite of the Insular Church.

Regional & Provincial Synods

The common Regional Synod addresses matters touching the whole Insular Church. Provincial and local synods may address matters particular to Ireland, Scotland, Wales, or England, while preserving the unity of the common constitution.

Conciliar Reception

The Church receives Nicaea I through Nicaea II, Lateran I through Lateran IV, and Constance. These councils provide its principal received ecumenical and medieval conciliar framework.

Constance & the Providential State of Exception

The extraordinary principle witnessed at Constance belongs to emergency ecclesiology, not ordinary government. Where the normal exercise of papal authority becomes gravely impossible or doubtful and the Church's visible survival is endangered, extraordinary conciliar action may be employed to preserve the faith, lawful succession, and unity until a recognisable Petrine order is restored. With the Papacy restored, that state of exception is presently inactive.

Mission of the Isles

The proper territorial and cultural focus of the Church remains Ireland, Scotland, Wales, and England. Mission beyond the Isles may be undertaken by synodal mandate without obscuring the Church's distinctive Insular character.
